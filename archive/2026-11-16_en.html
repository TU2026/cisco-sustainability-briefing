Cisco Sustainability Intelligence Briefing

Vietnam completes its renewable pricing architecture — regional PV price caps with storage premiums plus the country's first standalone BESS tariff framework — while a government resolution locks in market-reform deadlines, and COP31 puts record grid investment on the global agenda

Coverage window: November 9 – November 15, 2026 · Search language: English · Theme count: 5 · For: dit2@cisco.com

Executive Summary

Vietnam spent this week finishing the price architecture that its renewable build-out has been missing. On November 5, MOIT's Decision 2946/QĐ-BCT took effect, setting 2026 generation price ceilings for ground-mounted and floating solar by region — from ₫1,104.32/kWh in the south to ₫1,591.02/kWh in the north for ground PV — with higher ceilings (up to ₫2,120.31/kWh for floating PV with storage in the north) for projects pairing batteries at a minimum of 10% of plant capacity and 2 hours of storage duration. Caps rose 9.1%–16.8% across regions versus 2025, a clear signal that regulators want the stalled pipeline moving again. Then on November 11, Decision 2987/QĐ-BCT went further than any prior framework: for the first time, standalone (non-paired) battery storage got its own generation-service price — a ceiling of ₫3,209.98/kWh (~US$0.12) for systems of 10 MW or more connecting at 110 kV or above, priced on full life-cycle cost with an internal-rate-of-return cap of 12%.

The same government resolution that anchors Vietnam's energy-security program, Resolution 361/NQ-CP signed November 10, turned these pricing reforms into a dated reform calendar: competitive wholesale market design completed by December 2026, retail market design by June 2027, a two-part retail tariff study reported to the Prime Minister within 2026, and a renewable energy certificate (REC) market roadmap and legal framework developed across 2026–2027 — plus preferential credit lines for companies investing in storage and clean energy. Meanwhile at COP31 in Antalya, the world's utilities reported a record 21,000 km of new grid built in 2025 (50% more than 2024) and raised annual investment plans across grids, renewables and storage to at least US$156 billion, while IRENA's analysis put the cumulative global grid investment need at US$10 trillion by 2035. The through-line for Cisco: Vietnam has moved from policy intent to bankable price signals for storage; the remaining bottlenecks are market design and grid capacity — both now on dated countdowns.

Top 3 Actionable Intelligence
1 · Vietnam completes its renewable pricing architecture: regional PV price caps with storage premiums (Nov 5) plus the first-ever standalone BESS tariff (Nov 11)
Impact 9.0
Two parallel MOIT decisions now define how Vietnamese solar and storage projects get priced. Decision 2946/QĐ-BCT (effective November 5) sets 2026 generation price ceilings by region and type: ground-mounted PV from ₫1,104.32/kWh (south) to ₫1,591.02/kWh (north), floating PV from ₫1,340.21 to ₫1,939.74/kWh, and higher ceilings for projects paired with BESS — up to ₫1,813.48/kWh (ground) and ₫2,120.31/kWh (floating) in the north. Storage-paired eligibility requires capacity of at least 10% of the PV plant and 2 hours of storage/discharge duration. Versus the 2025 framework, caps rose 15.1% (north), 16.8% (center) and 9.1% (south). Decision 2987/QĐ-BCT (effective November 11) then created the first standalone BESS generation-service price framework: a ceiling of ₫3,209.98/kWh (~US$0.12, ex-VAT) for systems ≥10 MW connecting at ≥110 kV, with actual prices calculated per Circular 62/2025/TT-BCT on full life-cycle investment and O&M cost and an IRR capped at 12%. Context: Vietnam's solar fleet passed 19 GW cumulative by end-2025 but added only ~586 MW last year, and PDP8 targets 10,000–16,300 MW of storage by 2030 — these caps are the missing revenue basis for that gap.
Why it matters to Cisco: This converts Vietnam's storage story from pilot projects into a financeable asset class. For supplier engagements, the ceiling prices give a defensible basis for the solar-plus-storage business cases in the FY27 supplier energy roadmap — the north/center/south spread (a ~44% difference for ground PV) also changes which supplier sites have the best solar economics, so site rankings in the roadmap should be re-sorted. The standalone framework's ≥10 MW / ≥110 kV threshold means most individual factories won't qualify directly — the practical path is PV+BESS bundled projects or industrial-park aggregated storage, which is exactly the model being discussed with Foxconn and in the SCC cohort work.

2 · Resolution 361/NQ-CP locks Vietnam's market reform into dated deadlines: wholesale market by Dec 2026, retail design by Jun 2027, REC market roadmap 2026–27, and a two-part retail tariff study due to the PM this year
Impact 8.5
Signed November 10 by Permanent Deputy Prime Minister, Resolution 361/NQ-CP amends the National Energy Security Action Program (attached to Resolution 328/NQ-CP, implementing Politburo Resolution 70-NQ/TW, horizon 2030 with vision to 2045). The amendment adds institutional deadlines with teeth: MOIT must complete the review of the competitive wholesale electricity market design by December 2026 and the competitive retail market design by June 2027; a two-part retail tariff study must be reported to the Prime Minister within 2026; and a roadmap and legal framework for a renewable energy certificate (REC) market must be developed across 2026–2027, while EVN prepares a smart-grid development plan for 2026–2030 (vision 2045). The resolution also directs preferential investment credit policies for companies in green/clean energy, new technologies, energy-storage investment and domestic energy equipment manufacturing, tasks MOIT to draft a Prime Minister decision supporting household rooftop solar plus storage, requires a carbon credit exchange mechanism and domestic carbon market development in 2025–2026 with international linkage, and establishes strict handling mechanisms for investors who register energy projects but delay implementation through 2030.
Why it matters to Cisco: Three of these threads hit supplier decarbonization directly. First, a formal REC market roadmap means Vietnam-based suppliers may soon have a credible domestic instrument for renewable claims — replacing today's ad-hoc DPPA-attribute arrangements; build REC readiness into the FY27 supplier Scope 2 playbook. Second, the two-part retail tariff study (report due end-2026) will decide how the double-charging risk flagged for DPPA users gets resolved — a structural variable in every supplier PPA model. Third, preferential credit for storage investors lowers supplier financing costs, strengthening the solar+BESS business case from the previous item. The carbon-market linkage deadline (2025–2026 window) also matters for supplier carbon-credit quality in PCF and Scope 3 narratives.
Sources: 越南中文社 (Vietnam Chinese Community News) ↗
3 · COP31 Energy Day: utilities deliver a record 21,000 km of grid in 2025 and lift annual investment plans to US$156B — IRENA says global grid investment must reach US$10 trillion cumulatively by 2035
Impact 8.2
Announced at COP31 in Antalya on November 10 (Energy Day), members of the Utilities for Net Zero Alliance (UNEZA) — 89 utilities co-chaired by TAQA and SSE — reported building more than 21,000 km of new grid in 2025, 50% more than the 14,000 km delivered in 2024, and raised combined annual investment plans across grids, renewables and storage to at least US$156 billion (5% higher than last year, 35% higher than 2024), putting the alliance on track to mobilize more than US$1 trillion in energy-transition investment by 2030. UNEZA also formally endorsed the COP31 Presidency's "35-by-35" ambition — raising global electrification to 35% by 2035 — and earlier joint statements flagged a need for close to 90,000 km of transmission cable by 2030. IRENA's Director-General framed the stakes: global grid investment must more than double, reaching a cumulative US$10 trillion by 2035, for the transition to hold. For Southeast Asia specifically, grid build-out remains the binding constraint on both cross-border trade (the ASEAN Power Grid agenda) and domestic renewable absorption.
Why it matters to Cisco: The grid is now the bottleneck asset of the energy transition — and the region's utilities are spending accordingly. This supports Cisco's smart-grid, energy-management and IoT positioning with utility and industrial customers, and it matters internally: supplier factories competing for grid connections face queues that on-site solar plus BESS can bypass — another argument for the storage templates above. The US$10T grid-investment figure and the 35-by-35 electrification pledge are also strong anchor statistics for customer briefings and the FY27 narrative connecting Cisco's networking portfolio to electrification and grid modernization.

Strategic Recommendations

Short-Term (0–3 months)

  • Re-sort the supplier solar+storage site ranking: the new regional caps create a ~44% spread for ground PV between north (₫1,591.02) and south (₫1,104.32) — update the FY27 supplier energy roadmap's site-level economics to the Nov 5 caps, including the BESS premium eligibility test (10% / 2h).
  • Structure the next supplier BESS conversation around Decision 2987: use the ₫3,209.98/kWh ceiling and 12% IRR cap as the reference frame for factory or industrial-park storage proposals — and note the ≥10 MW / ≥110 kV threshold steers most factories toward bundled PV+BESS or aggregated park-level projects (Foxconn NbS/PPA workshop is the natural venue).
  • Track the two-part retail tariff study (due to the PM within 2026): its outcome determines how the DPPA double-charging risk resolves — flag it as a key assumption in any supplier PPA model built this quarter.
  • Deploy the grid-investment narrative: use the IRENA/UNEZA figures (21,000 km, US$156B/yr, US$10T by 2035, 35-by-35) in customer briefings and internal materials linking Cisco's networking portfolio to grid modernization and electrification.

Mid-Term (3–12 months)

  • Build REC-market readiness into the FY27 supplier Scope 2 playbook: with Resolution 361 mandating a REC market roadmap and legal framework across 2026–27, prepare supplier guidance for verifying and claiming Vietnamese RECs as the domestic instrument matures.
  • Prepare for merchant price exposure from the wholesale market reform (Dec 2026): as competitive market design lands, supplier PPAs will increasingly need shape, volume and hedge clauses — add these to the supplier energy contracting toolkit before the retail design lands in June 2027.
  • Position for the carbon-market linkage window (2025–2026): screen supplier carbon-credit quality against Vietnam's emerging domestic exchange mechanism before it connects to international markets — relevant to PCF and Scope 3 claims integrity.
  • Leverage preferential credit lines in supplier financing conversations: Resolution 361 directs concessional credit for storage and green-energy investors — a cost-of-capital lever that improves supplier BESS ROI; document it in the solar+BESS template as a financing note.
