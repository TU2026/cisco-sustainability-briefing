Cisco Sustainability Intelligence Briefing

Vietnam's Electricity Law rewrite, ASEAN Power Grid acceleration, and Cisco's dynamic product carbon footprint for a decarbonizing grid

Coverage window: September 22 – September 28, 2026 · Search language: English · Theme count: 5 · For: dit2@cisco.com

Executive Summary

Vietnam's power-market architecture is being rewritten again. On 21 September 2026 the National Assembly Standing Committee reviewed a draft law amending 23 articles of the 2024 Electricity Law, adding competitive bidding and bid-winning electricity prices, opening transmission investment to PPP and private capital, and putting offshore wind, battery storage and direct power purchase agreements (DPPA) on a clearer legal footing. In the same week, EVN Hanoi commissioned the country's first utility-scale battery energy storage system (BESS) — 50 MW / 100 MWh across five 110 kV substations — while Ho Chi Minh City's People's Council debated how to make DPPA transmission feasible by leasing the national grid rather than forcing every buyer and seller to build dedicated lines.

At the regional level, the ASEAN Power Grid moved from planning slides to policy pressure at Enlit Asia 2026 in Jakarta. Energy leaders pressed for faster action on a $764 billion regional grid build-out, even as Keppel unveiled plans for a 2 GW solar-plus-BESS project in Indonesia's Riau Islands to supply Singapore via subsea cable. On the Cisco front, the company published a new dynamic product carbon footprint methodology that adjusts hardware emissions for real-world grid decarbonization, backed by IEA data and a Fraunhofer review. The development arrives the same week the TNFD reported that nature-related disclosures have doubled to more than 1,000 organisations and released sector guidance asking data centre and semiconductor firms to screen water and land risks at the site level.

Top 3 Actionable Intelligence
1 · Vietnam revises Electricity Law to expand DPPA, open transmission to private capital, and put BESS and offshore wind on a firmer legal footing
Impact 9.0
On 21 September 2026 the Standing Committee of Vietnam's National Assembly reviewed the Draft Law on Amendments and Supplements to the Electricity Law. The draft revises 23 articles, repeals three administrative procedures and adds one, and signals a shift from approving projects to pricing risk: it introduces competitive investor selection with a bid-winning electricity price mechanism, allows transmission projects to use the PPP model to attract private capital, and explicitly covers offshore wind, energy storage and direct power purchase agreements (DPPA). The existing Electricity Law already introduced a two-part tariff combining capacity and energy prices; the amendments refine cross-subsidy exit paths and supportive price mechanisms for renewables, storage and social-capital transmission. HCMC's People's Council, meeting on 22 September, debated a draft resolution to expand DPPA participation and urged a shift away from forcing each transaction to build a dedicated transmission line toward leasing or sharing national grid infrastructure with multi-circuit technology. Meanwhile, EVN Hanoi completed installation and commissioning in September of five grid-connected BESS units totalling 50 MW / 100 MWh at 110 kV substations, and the Ministry of Industry and Trade is studying time-of-use pricing for residential and industrial customers to manage peak demand.
Cisco Relevance: Cisco's EMS/ODM suppliers in Bắc Ninh, Hải Phòng, Đồng Nai and Bình Dương now face a legal environment where DPPA, BESS co-location and private transmission investment are all explicitly on the table. Action window: Ask Vietnam procurement teams to require DPPA eligibility, grid-leasing options and BESS co-location in every new factory and rooftop-solar RFP, and to model power costs under the emerging two-part tariff and competitive-bidding rules rather than the flat retail tariff.

2 · ASEAN Power Grid acceleration at Enlit Asia 2026; Keppel plans 2 GW Indonesia–Singapore solar-plus-BESS subsea link
Impact 8.8
Regional energy leaders meeting at Enlit Asia 2026 in Jakarta (22–24 September) pressed for faster implementation of the ASEAN Power Grid (APG). The ASEAN initiative, first envisioned in 1997 and targeted for completion by 2045, is estimated to require $764 billion in generation and transmission investment, with the World Bank putting the figure at roughly $800 billion. ASEAN currently has about 7.7 GW of cross-border interconnection capacity and aims to double that by 2040. Demand is being driven by data centres — some requiring more than 1 GW — and electric vehicles. Thai regulator Warit Rattanachuen stressed that countries must first secure domestic power security before deepening cross-border trade. In parallel, Keppel disclosed on 19 September a plan to connect a 2 GW solar project in Indonesia's Riau Islands, paired with a utility-scale BESS and subsea cable, to its Keppel Merlimau Cogen plant on Singapore's Jurong Island. Keppel already holds a conditional EMA licence to import 300 MW of low-carbon electricity from Indonesia. Singapore is targeting roughly 6 GW of low-carbon electricity imports by 2035, with six companies licensed to import 3 GW from Indonesia and seven others conditionally approved for a combined 6.25 GW. Indonesia's five-year export-permit renewal requirement remains a key regulatory uncertainty for developers.
Cisco Relevance: A functioning APG and the Indonesia–Singapore corridor could open cross-border renewable PPAs and REC structures for Cisco supplier clusters in Vietnam and Thailand, reducing reliance on national grid decarbonization alone. Action window: Track the Singapore 6 GW import roadmap and Indonesia's export regulations; ask regional energy advisors to model cross-border wheeling scenarios for Foxconn/NIDEC/AVC supplier parks in southern Vietnam.

3 · Cisco unveils dynamic product carbon footprint methodology tied to real-world grid decarbonization
Impact 8.5
Cisco published a blog post on 22 September 2026 explaining its evolution from static to dynamic product carbon footprints (PCFs). The methodology identifies hotspots — printed circuit boards, integrated circuits and test/assembly energy — isolates the portion of emissions attributable to supplier electricity consumption, and adjusts those emissions using reputable global grid data and IEA projections. This allows Cisco to backcast historical PCFs and forecast future reductions as production grids get cleaner. Fraunhofer has critically reviewed Cisco's static PCF methodology for alignment with ISO 14067 and is planned to review the dynamic calculation as well. PCF data is being made available to partners through Cisco's price list API and to customers through the Product Sustainability API. The approach is designed to help customers track Scope 3 progress, set net-zero targets and make procurement decisions that reflect the carbon trajectory of identical products purchased in different years.
Cisco Relevance: Dynamic PCFs give Cisco a concrete lever to convert supplier renewable electricity and DPPA progress into measurable Scope 3 reductions, directly supporting the FY30 Scope 3 -30% target and Net Zero 2040 goal. Action window: Integrate the Product Sustainability API into supplier engagement so that key EMS/ODM partners can see how grid decarbonization in Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia lowers the carbon footprint of Cisco hardware.

Strategic Recommendations

Short term (0–3 months)

  • Vietnam supplier DPPA/BESS playbook: Brief Foxconn, Pegatron, NIDEC and AVC procurement teams on the revised Electricity Law; require DPPA eligibility, national-grid leasing options and VPP-ready control interfaces in all new rooftop-solar and storage RFPs, and model power costs under the bid-winning price and two-part tariff.
  • ASEAN Power Grid watchlist: Assign a regional energy advisor to track Enlit Asia follow-ups, Indonesia's export-regulation timeline and Singapore's 6 GW import roadmap, with an initial cross-border wheeling scenario for southern-Vietnam supplier parks.
  • Dynamic PCF pilot with top suppliers: Use Cisco's Product Sustainability API to show five to ten key EMS/ODM suppliers how grid decarbonization assumptions affect product carbon footprints, and ask them to align their own renewable-energy procurement with the methodology.

Medium term (3–12 months)

  • Regional low-carbon procurement cluster: Evaluate whether Cisco can join or sponsor a buyer cohort for cross-border renewable imports into Vietnam/Thailand, leveraging Indonesia–Singapore and Laos–Thailand–Malaysia–Singapore corridors to add firmness to supplier PPAs.
  • CDP Water Security Module 5.11 SOP finalisation: Integrate the TNFD tech-sector site-level water/land screening, Water Scopes 1–3 boundary logic and Singapore/Thailand water-recycling benchmarks into the supplier assessment SOP, with explicit thresholds for fabs in water-stressed basins.
  • Nature and carbon disclosure roadmap: Prepare a TNFD/ISSB-aligned nature and water risk disclosure roadmap that references the 1,002-organisation TNFD momentum, dynamic PCF pilot results and the ISSB nature exposure draft expected in October 2026.
