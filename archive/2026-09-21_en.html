Vietnam's first grid-scale BESS + VPP framework, Singapore–Thailand ASEAN Power Grid push, and Cisco FY25 water progress against Water Scopes 1–3

Coverage window: September 15 – September 21, 2026 · Search language: English · Theme count: 5 · For: dit2@cisco.com

Executive Summary

Vietnam's power system moved from generation-only headlines to grid-integration execution this week. EVN Hanoi commissioned the country's first utility-scale battery energy storage system (BESS) — 50 MW / 100 MWh across five 110 kV substations — while the Ministry of Industry and Trade began drafting a policy framework for Virtual Power Plants (VPPs) that would aggregate rooftop solar, behind-the-meter BESS and electric vehicles as flexible grid resources. These developments arrive as the first private-wire Direct Power Purchase Agreement (DPPA) projects come online, including Samsung Electronics' 28 MWp rooftop solar installation in Ho Chi Minh City, which will supply more than 40 GWh annually and cut approximately 26,000 tonnes of CO₂ per year.

At the regional level, Singapore and Thailand announced a two-year ASEAN chairmanship push (2027–2028) to unblock regulatory barriers to the ASEAN Power Grid, with submarine-cable rules, wheeling charges and export-tax harmonisation as the immediate priorities. The announcement reinforces the 3.4 GW Indonesia–Singapore cross-border solar export framework now in advanced negotiations. On water and disclosure, Cisco's FY25 Purpose Report confirms an 11% reduction in direct-operational water withdrawals and deeper supply-chain participation in the Alliance for Water Stewardship Collective Action Accelerator, while the WRI-led Water Scopes 1–3 framework continues to mature as the value-chain water equivalent of the GHG Protocol.

Top 3 Actionable Intelligence
1 · Vietnam commissions first utility-scale BESS and begins VPP rule-making, while the first private-wire DPPA rooftops enter commercial operation
Impact 9.0
On 16 September 2026 EVN Hanoi officially commissioned Vietnam's first large-scale grid-connected BESS: 50 MW / 100 MWh split across five 110 kV substations in Hanoi (Bắc Thang Long, Quang Minh, Sài Đồng 2, Phùng Xá and Thanh Oai). The pilot stores electricity during low-demand periods and discharges at peak, giving the grid the flexibility needed to absorb more variable renewable generation. In parallel, Vietnam's Ministry of Industry and Trade published an industry-agency discussion paper laying early groundwork for Virtual Power Plants, which would coordinate distributed rooftop solar, behind-the-meter BESS and electric vehicles as dispatchable resources. The policy discussion is driven by rising demand — national electricity consumption exceeded 203 billion kWh in the first seven months of 2026, up 9.3% year-on-year, with peak load hitting a record 58.4 GW — and by continuing curtailment in central and southern regions. Vietnam's revised Power Development Plan now requires many new utility-scale solar projects to pair with BESS equivalent to at least 10% of installed capacity and two hours of duration. The first DPPA projects are also now live: Samsung Electronics inaugurated a 28 MWp rooftop solar array at its Ho Chi Minh City manufacturing complex under a private-wire DPPA with TotalEnergies ENEOS, expected to generate more than 40 GWh/year and avoid ~26,000 tonnes of CO₂ annually.
Cisco Relevance: Cisco's EMS/ODM suppliers in Bắc Ninh, Hải Phòng, Đồng Nai and Bình Dương now have a live regulatory and technical path to combine rooftop solar, behind-the-meter BESS and DPPAs — the exact bundle needed to decarbonise electronics manufacturing. Action window: Ask key Vietnam suppliers to include VPP-ready controls and BESS co-location in any new rooftop-solar or DPPA term sheets, and reference Samsung's 28 MWp DPPA as a bankable precedent.

2 · Singapore and Thailand launch a two-year ASEAN Power Grid regulatory push; 3.4 GW Indonesia–Singapore cross-border solar framework advances
Impact 8.7
At the Unlocking Capital for Sustainability summit in Singapore on 17 September 2026, Second Minister for Finance Jeffrey Siow announced that Singapore (2027 ASEAN chair) and Thailand (2028 chair) will use their consecutive chairmanships to tackle the regulatory barriers delaying the ASEAN Power Grid. The immediate priorities are the ASEAN Submarine Power Cable Development Framework, harmonisation of wheeling charges and export taxes, and a regional blueprint for subsea cables. The IEA estimates that more than US$300 billion is needed to expand and modernise Southeast Asian grids between 2025 and 2040, with cross-border interconnectors alone requiring about US$27 billion by 2040 — yet only about US$2 billion has been invested in such links over five decades. ASEAN had 2.8 GW of operational grid-to-grid interconnection capacity in 2024, with 13.7 GW more planned by 2040. Six future submarine interconnections have been identified, four of which would exceed the length of the current world's longest operational subsea link (Viking Link). This regulatory momentum reinforces the 3.4 GW Indonesia–Singapore cross-border solar export framework announced on 3 September 2026, under which PV-plus-BESS projects in Indonesia's Riau Islands would supply roughly 30% of Singapore's projected electricity demand by 2035.
Cisco Relevance: A functioning ASEAN Power Grid would allow Cisco's supplier clusters in Vietnam and Thailand to source renewable electrons from regional low-cost generation hubs, creating new cross-border PPA and REC structures beyond national boundaries. Action window: Track the Singapore–Thailand working-group outputs through 2027 and ask regional energy advisors to model cross-border wheeling scenarios for Foxconn/NIDEC/AVC supplier parks in Vietnam.

3 · Cisco FY25 Purpose Report shows 11% direct water withdrawal reduction; Water Scopes 1–3 and TNFD-aligned nature disclosure mature as value-chain standards
Impact 8.5
Cisco's FY25 Purpose Report, published 2 June 2026, states that the company reduced water withdrawals for direct operations by 11% in fiscal 2025 and advanced supply-chain participation in the Alliance for Water Stewardship (AWS) Collective Action Accelerator. Cisco has adopted the Responsible Business Alliance (RBA) Code of Conduct as its Supplier Code of Conduct, which includes explicit water-stewardship requirements, and aligns its water approach with UN SDG 6 and the AWS framework. These disclosures arrive as the WRI-led "Corporate Guidance for Assessing Water Scopes 1–3 in Value Chains" — co-developed with WWF, SCS Global Services and the CEO Water Mandate — progresses toward final guidance expected Q4 2027. The framework aims to create a GHG-Protocol-style common language for water dependencies, impacts, risks and opportunities (DIROs) across value chains, connecting to CDP, GRI, TNFD and CSRD disclosure requirements. Separately, TNFD-aligned nature reporting is being institutionalised through the ISSB, which is expected to release an exposure draft of nature-related disclosure requirements in October 2026.
Cisco Relevance: The 11% operational water reduction and AWS supply-chain accelerator provide concrete proof points for Cisco's CDP Water Security Module 5.11 Value Chain Engagement SOP, while Water Scopes 1–3 offers the standardised boundary logic needed for Tier-1 supplier screening (NIDEC/Đồng Nai basin, AVC/Mã basin). Action window: Map FY26 supplier water-disclosure coverage against the draft Water Scopes 1–3 boundary categories now, so the SOP can be finalised before the Q4 2027 guidance release.
